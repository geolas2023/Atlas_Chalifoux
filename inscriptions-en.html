“B. Chalifou,” 1835: a name carved by another hand

Jean-Baptiste Chalifoux could not write. Yet his name appears on a rock face along Willow Creek, in Utah's Book Cliffs, in a careful Spanish hand, next to that of Juan Valdez, who probably carved it. These few letters are one of the rare dated traces of his trapping years in the Ute country.

Valdez and Chalifou (1835) Antoine Leroux (1835) outline only: approximate or hypothetical position — probable route of 1827, from Taos to the Ute country

A carved trail

The Westwater-Willow Creek trail, the north branch of the Old Spanish Trail, climbed from the Westwater canyons by Hay Canyon, crossed the Book Cliffs near present-day Three Pines, then went down Wire Fence Canyon to Willow Creek, which the trail followed for some seventy kilometers to the Green, near Ouray. It was the route of the Taos traders to Antoine Robidoux's Fort Uintah. J. W. Nelson records a series of inscriptions along it: Antoine Robidoux at Westwater (1837), Louis Robidoux at the mouth of Wire Fence Canyon (1841), “ARobidoux,” A' Leroux (1835) and, below the Pine Springs access, “Juan Valdez Paso 8 & 9 N 9 1835,” “F.R.B.” and “B. Chalifou.”

The entries

Why the positions are approximate

The exact locations of these inscriptions are not published. The points are placed according to Nelson's descriptions and the official place names (GNIS): the mouth of Pine Spring Canyon for Valdez and Chalifou, a little downstream for Leroux. The inscriptions of the Robidoux and of Denis Julien are gathered on the page of the Robidoux Atlas (in French).
